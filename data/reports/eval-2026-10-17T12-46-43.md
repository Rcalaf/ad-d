**Answer:** A thief can use the backstab ability to gain a +4 bonus to hit and to negate the target's shield and Dexterity bonuses when attacking someone by surprise from behind. However, the backstab is only effective if the victim is unaware of the attack, and the thief must be able to reach a significant target area. The backstabbing does not apply to every creature; it is limited to generally humanoid targets with a definable back. ## 9. charging (rule, draft)

**Question:** What are the effects of charging an opponent?

**Expected:** The charging character gets a bonus to the attack roll but loses Dexterity bonuses to Armor Class and suffers an Armor Class penalty; a set spear or polearm against a charge does double damage.

**Answer:** A character who charges an opponent gains a +2 bonus to their attack roll and can make an attack at the end of their movement. However, they suffer a -2 penalty to their initiative roll, lose Dexterity bonuses to Armor Class, and take a -1 penalty to their Armor Class. Additionally, if the defender is using a spear or polearm and sets it against the charge, they can deal double damage on a successful hit.

## 11. ability-score-method (rule, draft)
